Help guide

1 · What you need
  • A keyboard with a USB port (or an older 5‑pin MIDI keyboard plus a MIDI‑to‑USB adapter).
  • The cable: a standard USB‑A to USB‑B cable, the same square-ended type many printers use.
  • A laptop, or an Android phone with a USB‑OTG adapter (USB‑C or micro‑USB to USB‑A female).
  • Chrome, Edge or Opera. iPhone and iPad Safari can’t receive MIDI in web pages, but the on‑screen keys still work.
2 · Connect the keyboard

Laptop:

  1. Switch the keyboard on and plug the USB‑B end into the keyboard, the other end into the laptop.
  2. Open StagePatch in Chrome or Edge and go to Play.
  3. Press Connect MIDI keyboard and choose Allow.
  4. The dot at the top turns green and says how many devices are connected. Press a key: you should hear sound.

Android phone:

  1. Plug the OTG adapter into the phone, then the USB cable from the adapter to the keyboard.
  2. Open StagePatch in Chrome, press Connect MIDI keyboard and Allow.
  3. If the phone asks what to do with the USB device, choose to use it for MIDI or just dismiss it.

Older 5‑pin MIDI keyboards: plug a MIDI‑to‑USB interface into the keyboard’s MIDI OUT port, then connect it to the phone or laptop as above.

If you have several devices, pick your keyboard in the list next to the Connect button, or leave it on All MIDI inputs.

3 · Build a song patch

A song is a set of six sections. Each section has its own patch: one or more sounds layered together.

  1. Go to Play. Under Song, press + New and type a name.
  2. Tap a section button, for example Intro.
  3. Under Sounds in this section, choose a sound from the list (10 each of piano, electric piano, organ, strings, pad, brass, lead, bass, pluck and bells).
  4. Press + Add another sound to layer more, for example piano plus strings.
  5. Use each sound’s Volume slider to balance them. −8va / +8va moves a sound one octave down or up.
  6. Plays on lets you split the keyboard: put a bass on the Left half only and a piano on the Right half only. The split is at middle C.
  7. The 🔊 On button mutes a sound without deleting it. ✕ removes it.
  8. Move to the next section and repeat. Copy from previous section is a quick starting point.
  9. Test by playing keys, and tap sections to switch like you would on stage.
4 · What each section is for
  • Intro: the opening. Try a soft pad or piano.
  • Verse: the storytelling part. Keep it simple, such as piano or electric piano.
  • Pre-Chorus: builds energy. Add strings and a little more volume.
  • Chorus: the big part. Layer piano, strings and a bass on the left half.
  • Instrumental: the solo break. A synth lead, brass or bells work well.
  • Bridge: a change of mood. Try organ with a warm pad.

These are only ideas. Every section can use any sounds.

5 · Shape a sound (attack, release, cutoff)

Under any sound, press 🎛 Tweak this sound. Move a slider, then play a key to hear it. A dot ● on the button means that sound has been changed. Back to original sound undoes it.

  • Attack: how slowly the note fades in. Low is instant (piano), high is a slow swell (strings, pads).
  • Decay: how fast the note dips after the first hit.
  • Sustain: how loud the note stays while you hold the key. Low makes plucky sounds, high makes organ-like sounds.
  • Release: how long the sound rings after you let go. High gives a long tail.
  • Cutoff: low is soft and dark, high is bright and sharp.
  • Resonance: adds a ringing, whistling edge around the cutoff. Keep it low for a natural sound.

Settings are saved separately for each sound in each section.

6 · Playing live
  • Tap a section button to switch patches. On a laptop you can also press 1–6.
  • Your keyboard’s Program Change buttons 1–6 also switch the sections, and a sustain pedal works.
  • Master volume and Room / echo are under Overall. Stop all clears any stuck note.
7 · Save and load your songs
  • Your songs are kept on this device automatically.
  • For a backup file, go to Play, scroll to Save & load songs and press Save this song to a file (or Save all songs). Confirm the download.
  • To bring songs back, or move them to another phone or laptop, press Load a song file and pick the file. The songs are added to your list.
8 · Troubleshooting
  • No sound: turn up the phone or laptop volume and the Master volume, and tap the screen once so the browser lets the page play audio.
  • Keyboard not found: check the cable, switch the keyboard on, press Connect again, and use Chrome or Edge. Some keyboards need to be set to USB MIDI mode.
  • Stuck note: press Stop all.
  • Delay: close other apps and use a wired connection, not Bluetooth headphones.

How to connect

  • Laptop (Chrome, Edge or Opera): plug the keyboard in with its USB cable (the “printer” type USB‑B cable, or a MIDI‑to‑USB adapter), press Connect MIDI keyboard and allow access.
  • Android phone (Chrome): use a USB‑OTG adapter + the same cable.
  • iPhone/iPad: Safari doesn’t support MIDI in web pages, so use the on‑screen keys or a MIDI‑capable browser app.
  • Everything is free; all sounds are generated live, no downloads. Songs also save on this device automatically; use “Save this song to a file” to keep a backup or move it to another device, then “Load a song file” to bring it back.
